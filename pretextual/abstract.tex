\begin{abstract}
This research addresses the need to produce synthetic cyber risk data that are
sufficiently faithful and useful for analysis, without assuming that synthetic
generation, by itself, ensures privacy or analytical validity. The
methodological pipeline will be organized in six stages: exploratory analysis
and data preparation; synthetic generation with CTGAN and, when feasible, a
non-neural baseline; fidelity evaluation; analytical utility evaluation;
exposure risk evaluation; and synthesis of the results and definition of the
conditions of use. Utility will be evaluated through the TSTR protocol, compared
with TRTR, on an analytical task defined a priori. The artifact will be
considered technically promising if it simultaneously presents evidence of
fidelity, analytical utility adequate to the defined workload, and absence of
relevant signs of exposure.
\end{abstract}
